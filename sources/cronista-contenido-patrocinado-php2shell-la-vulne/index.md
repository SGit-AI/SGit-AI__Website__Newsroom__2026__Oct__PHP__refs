#### What the page says

_Curator's reading of the capture._

A sponsored-content article on El Cronista (Argentina, 29 October) from an IT asset-management vendor, arguing that PHP2Shell exposes the blind spot of IT assets: companies do not know where PHP runs in their own infrastructure. It restates the patched versions (8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, 8.2.35), the comparison with Log4Shell, Palo Alto's count of more than 22.6 million exposed potentially vulnerable instances, and that Linux distributions such as Ubuntu incorporate the fix without changing PHP's version number, so a spreadsheet can mislead; it reports that Colombia's national response team issued a critical alert and counted over 8,600 PHP services exposed, then explains how the vendor's own platform finds affected assets.

> Distribuciones de Linux como Ubuntu incorporan la corrección sin cambiar el número de versión original de PHP.

_Curator's translation:_ "Linux distributions such as Ubuntu incorporate the fix without changing PHP's original version number."
