#### What the page says

_Curator's reading of the capture._

A UK tabloid explainer (Metro, 22 October) asking how worried ordinary people should be about the PHP flaws. It says about 70% of websites use PHP, that the PHP Foundation announced on Tuesday an update for the following day, that security agencies 'from the UK and the US to China and Japan' called on websites to go dark if they cannot update, and that UK ministers took 32 government services down that day to carry out the fix and announced a 'Be Ready' campaign. It quotes experts: a worry that hackers could reverse-engineer the patches before sites update, a Malwarebytes researcher that attackers can hide behind trusted sites and that a hacked shop's check-out could be edited to skim cards, and a media company's security head that there was still no definite proof the holes had been exploited, so no reason to stop browsing, only to beware suspicious messages. It reports Calif's discovery, OpenAI's remark that the bugs are among the most impactful security issues in recent memory, researchers' reports of 'millions' of attempts, and that the Internet Archive said it was 'attacked' the day before and is back up.
