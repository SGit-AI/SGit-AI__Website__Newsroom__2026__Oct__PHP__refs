#### What the page says

_Curator's reading of the capture._

A community-forum recap (Spiceworks, 30 October) of how members of its IT community reacted to PHP2Shell. It says that hours before the fix a sysadmin saw advance notice of the release on the php.net homepage and posted it, with leaked details the PHP team had not confirmed, to the Spiceworks Community, giving readers a head start while European organisations were already taking sites offline; that members stressed WordPress updates are not PHP updates, that installing an update is not running it until services restart, and that appliances, admin portals, backup devices and printers may run PHP; and that a vulnerable version does not prove a break-in but rotating credentials after exposure is cheap. It relays that the European Commission, ENISA, CERT-EU and Europol urged immediate action and warned products may be affected even if they do not appear to use PHP directly, that Amazon told defenders to look for evidence from 17 October, that Unit 42 could not confirm that an attack on a European government agency used the flaws and Sophos saw no exploitation in its customer telemetry, and that AI has shortened the time to exploit new flaws, in its words, from about a week to 24 to 48 hours.

> WordPress updates are not the same thing as PHP updates.
